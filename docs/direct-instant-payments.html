Push Payments on BSV
Payments

Pay a person, not an address.

BSV wallets now hand a payment straight to the person you're paying. Phone to phone when you're together, wallet to wallet over the internet when you're apart, and through a message box when they're offline. This page explains how each way works, and shows openly what every device and server involved can and can't see.

Shipped in BSV Desktop 3.2.0 and BSV Browser 1.8.0 (iOS, Android) Open protocol @bsv/webrtpay Status live, confirmed device to device
Three ways to pay

The most private way that works, every time

Your wallet picks the way that reveals the least about you, given where the other person is. Each way down the list involves one more party, so it shares a little more.

1 · NearbyBest for privacy

Phone to phone, when you're together

  • You scan their payment code; the phones talk directly over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, or by animated QR codes.
  • No server carries the payment. Nothing leaves the room until the transaction is sent to the network.
  • Phones only (BSV Browser).
2 · LiveWhen you're apart

Wallet to wallet, over the internet

  • A relay introduces the two wallets; they then connect directly, encrypted end to end.
  • The relay sees who connects to whom and when, never the payment.
  • Phones and desktops, in any combination.
3 · Message boxWhen they're offline

Left for them to collect

  • The payment is sealed for the recipient and held until their wallet next opens.
  • The message box sees who paid whom and when, never the payment or amount.
  • Used only after you agree, when live delivery can't reach them.
One rule for all three

Privacy first: share only what's needed for the payment to arrive. In every way, the payment itself (the transaction, the amount, and the private details needed to spend it) is encrypted so only the recipient can read it. What differs is how much metadata the people and servers in between can see.

    The specification

    What happens when you press Pay

    This is what shipped in BSV Desktop and BSV Browser. Timings are the defaults the apps use.

    in person · phones

    Nearby: scan, then pay

    The person being paid shows a payment code. Scanning it gives your phone their identity, the amount and a one-time secret for this payment. Your phone then finds theirs over Apple's peer-to-peer Wi-Fi, Google's Nearby Connections or Bluetooth, trying the quickest first. If no radio works, it shows the payment as a short series of animated QR codes for their camera to read. Whichever path it takes, the payment is sealed with that one-time secret. Their phone checks it and replies "got it". Their phone then sends the transaction to the network as it takes the payment in, and yours sends the same transaction too.

    step 1 · you

    Apart: choose a person

    Search by name, handle, email or identity key and enter an amount. That's all you do. The wallet decides how the payment travels.

    step 2 · 0 to 2 seconds

    Knock

    Your wallet sends a small knock through the relay, encrypted so only the recipient's wallet can open it. If their wallet is open it answers with a ring, usually in well under a second. If not, the relay says so straight away. Your wallet waits at most 2 seconds before treating them as offline.

    step 3 · up to 10 seconds

    Connect

    The wallets open an encrypted WebRTC link. Each asks a STUN server for its public address; wallets on the same network use local addresses. If no direct path exists, for example on some mobile hotspots or strict office firewalls, the link is relayed by a TURN server. It stays encrypted end to end either way. Each wallet checks that the other end of the link really is the identity it expected.

    step 4 · about a second

    Pay

    Your wallet sends the payment over the link: the signed transaction and the private details the recipient needs to spend it. Their wallet checks the payment is really to them, sends the transaction to the network as it takes it in, and confirms. Your wallet then sends the same transaction too; the network already has it, so nothing is paid twice. One payment is always one transaction. If the link drops after the payment has left your wallet, the same payment finishes through the message box, never a second time.

    Success screen: "Paid live: it's already in their wallet."
    fallback · when they're not online

    Offer the message box

    When the recipient isn't online, or the direct link can't form and nothing was spent, you're asked first:

    "Sam isn't online right now. Send it to their message box? They'll get it next time they open their wallet."

    Say yes, and the same encrypted payment is left in their message box. A few seconds later your wallet sends the transaction to the network in the background. Their wallet collects the payment automatically the next time it opens, sends the transaction again as it takes it in, and the box deletes its copy. There's nothing for them to press.

    receiving · on by default

    Be payable

    While your wallet is open it stays reachable through the relay, and the Receive screen says "Open to live payments". On desktop that's while the wallet is unlocked. On a phone, it's while the app is in the foreground; the rest of the time payers are offered your message box. Every arrival shows a short notice. You can turn live receiving off in Settings at any time.

    For wallet builders

    Add live payments to your wallet

    The live protocol is open source in the BSV TypeScript stack. Your wallet needs:

    • the standard BSV wallet interface
    • a WebRTC implementation
    • a way to signal (the public relay works out of the box)

    Nearby payments ship in the BSV mobile toolkit.

    Install

    # browsers and Electron
npm install @bsv/webrtpay

# React Native: add the native WebRTC module
npm install @bsv/webrtpay react-native-webrtc

    Expo apps can use @bsv/expo-wallet-toolbox (0.19+). It includes the ready-made pieces:

    • live payments, with the pay flow, the Receive card and the message box fallback
    • nearby payments, through @bsv/react-native-localpay-transport

    Pay and be paid live

    const relay = new RelaySignaler({
  url: 'wss://webrtpay-relay-us-1.bsvblockchain.tech',
  wallet, originator
})
const client = new DirectPayClient({
  wallet, originator, chain: 'main',
  signaler: relay,
  rtc: { iceServers: [{ urls: 'stun:stun-us-1.bsvb.tech:3478' }] },
  iceServersProvider: relay.turnProvider()
})

// payee
await client.startReceiving({ onPayment })
// payer
const peer = await client.connect(payeeIdentityKey)
const ack = await client.sendPayment(peer, token)

    Abridged from the package README. onPayment checks the payment with verifyDirectPayment and takes it in once. The README covers retries and storage.

    Protocol at a glance

    • Presence: knock, then ring or reject, answered before any network address is shared.
    • Signalling: every message is encrypted for the other wallet; the relay sees only who it's for.
    • Identity: each wallet proves who it is as part of setting up the encrypted link.
    • Payment: the transaction with its proof history, plus the private spending details, checked by the recipient before it's accepted.
    • Errors: peer_unreachable, rejected, unavailable, timeout, so wallets can offer the right fallback.

    Run your own infrastructure

    • Relay: a stateless WebSocket service, published as a container image. Point clients at your own to keep presence metadata in your hands.
    • STUN and TURN: ordinary WebRTC servers. Use ours, your own, or a provider's.
    • Message box: can carry signalling as well as offline payments.

    Wallets on different relays can't find each other live, but message boxes still connect them.
